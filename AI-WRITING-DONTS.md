# 30 things that make text read as AI-generated

Every file in this repo, Hebrew or English, avoids all thirty. Each item gives the tell, an example, and what to write instead. Compiled from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide, Pangram's frequency data, Cherryleaf (February 2026) and Olivia Cal's list of AI writing tells (2026), plus the corrections made to this repo's own files.

## Headings and structure

1. Abstract "X and Y" headings. Two abstract nouns joined by "and" sound like a book chapter: "הפסגה והמחיר", "Legacy and impact", "Challenges and opportunities". Name the actual period or event instead: "העזיבה של Bringg", "החברה הראשונה".
2. Clever or poetic headings. Wordplay, alliteration or a mini-headline where a plain label works: "הילד מקריית אתא", "From zero to unicorn". Use the plain word: "ילדות".
3. A heading over every few lines. Short sections that each get a title, or headings that sit over a single paragraph. Merge them and keep headings for real sections.
4. Meta intros and outros. Lines about the document itself: "This document explains...", "המסמך הזה מפרט", "למי: צוות ההפקה". Start with the content and end when it ends.
5. Formulaic wrap-ups. "In conclusion", "Overall", "Bottom line", "לסיכום", or a last paragraph that repeats what came before. Stop after the last real point.
6. Templated parallel sections. Every section built the same way and ending with the same formula, such as "we learn X from him because Y". Let each section have the shape its content needs.
7. Uniform rhythm. Paragraphs of the same length, sentences of the same length, each paragraph ending with a neat summary line. Mix long and short sentences and stop paragraphs where the thought stops.

## Sentence patterns

8. Negative parallelism. "Not X but Y", "It's not just X, it's Y", "לא X אלא Y", "X ולא Y". Say the positive claim directly.
9. "No X. No Y. Just Z." Staccato triple fragments used as punchlines. Write a normal sentence.
10. Triads by habit. Three adjectives, three examples, three benefits, every time. Use as many items as the content really has.
11. Tacked-on analysis clauses. "..., highlighting X", "..., reflecting Y", "..., מה שמדגיש/ממחיש את". End the sentence, or make the point in its own sentence with a fact.
12. Avoiding plain "is". "Serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "מהווה". Write "is", "הוא", "היא".
13. Rhetorical questions as transitions. "So what does this mean?", "ומה זה אומר?" before the answer. State the answer.
14. Signposting. "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember", "חשוב לציין", "כדאי לזכור ש", "זה אומר ש". Delete the signpost and keep the fact.
15. Formal connectors at the start of sentences. "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Crucially", "יתרה מזאת", "בנוסף לכך", "מעבר לכך". Use "גם", "ו", or nothing.
16. Hedge stacking. "Often", "typically", "generally", "may", "עשוי", "ייתכן ש", "באופן כללי" in sentence after sentence. Commit, or give the source of the doubt once.

## Vocabulary

17. Stock AI words. English: delve, crucial, pivotal, tapestry, testament, showcase, landscape, realm, robust, seamless, leverage, navigate, foster, multifaceted, holistic, game-changer, unlock, elevate, journey. Hebrew: "מסע", "מרתק", "פורץ דרך", "מגוון רחב", "שלל", "בעולם של היום", "בעידן הנוכחי", "נדבך", "משמעותי ביותר". Use the plain word.
18. Inflated significance. "A turning point", "a testament to", "set the stage for", "left a lasting legacy", "רגע מכונן", "אבן דרך". Give the fact and let the reader judge its weight.
19. Promotional tone. "Vibrant", "world-class", "unique", "boasts", "מוביל", "ייחודי", "חסר תקדים". Describe what it is or does.
20. Global openers. "In today's fast-paced world", "In the digital age", "בעולם המשתנה של היום". Start with the subject.
21. Intensifiers that fake sincerity. "Genuinely", "truly", "actually", "באמת", "ממש", "לגמרי" in written text. Cut them. In quotes from a real person, keep their words.
22. Chatbot phrasing and fake intimacy. "Certainly!", "Great question", "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "I hope this helps", "הנה העניין". Never use them.
23. Synonym cycling. A new word each time for the same thing, such as "the company", "the startup", "the venture", "the firm". Reuse the same word.

## Formatting

24. Too much bold. Bold almost nothing; in a questions file, at most the one central question.
25. Inline-header lists. Bullets that each start with a bold label and a colon, such as "**Timing:** ...". Write the sentence.
26. Emoji as formatting. ✅ 🚀 🔥 ▶️ 📄 ⚠️ 🟢 and similar markers. Use words.
27. Unusual Unicode. Arrows (→ ← ↳), ≈, ⚠, box-drawing characters, decorative bullets such as "·", em dashes, and spaced en dashes used as em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a new sentence.
28. Lists and tables where prose fits. A table for two facts, or bullets that should be one sentence. Use a list only for items that really are a list.

## Content

29. Generic specificity and invented scenes. Examples that sound concrete but name no real person, number or date, and set-ups like "Picture this", "דמיינו ש...". Use a real case with names, numbers and a date, or leave it out.
30. Unverifiable or invented content. Claims with no source, "experts say", "studies show", "רבים טוענים", broken or invented links, and placeholders such as "XX" or "INSERT". Name the source or drop the claim, and check every link.
