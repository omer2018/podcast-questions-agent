# Question QA

A question is final only when it passes the 20 boxes in part 1. Part 2 breaks them into concrete checks for critiquing a question. Part 3 holds real edits from this project, before and after, with the reason for each. Add a new example to part 3 every time the user asks for a question to be revised.

## Part 1: the 20 boxes

- [ ] 1. One question. No second question hidden in the sentence or in a follow-up glued to it.
- [ ] 2. A stranger understands it. Someone who never heard of the guest knows from the question alone what is being asked and why the detail matters.
- [ ] 3. It follows from the question before it, or its context makes the jump clear. Nothing arrives out of the blue.
- [ ] 4. No hidden assumption. It takes for granted only what the guest has said in public.
- [ ] 5. The guest hasn't already answered it in a previous interview, or it goes a clear step past that answer.
- [ ] 6. Every fact is verified, attributed to the right person, and not stitched together from two sources.
- [ ] 7. Short. About 12 words of question, at most one short sentence of context, about 25 words in all.
- [ ] 8. Specific to this guest. Another founder could not answer it the same way.
- [ ] 9. It asks for one concrete thing: a scene, a decision, a feeling, a number. Never "what did you see", never a lesson.
- [ ] 10. Respectful. No personal wealth, no reported sums, nothing that mocks him or ambushes him with a peer.
- [ ] 11. Gentle with family. A parent's illness, a partner or children come up only as the guest has framed them in public, and only if the question needs them.
- [ ] 12. Answerable. He can answer it without breaking an NDA, military censorship or his employer's disclosure rules.
- [ ] 13. Grounded. It asks about his experience, not for a forecast of a disaster.
- [ ] 14. Spoken Hebrew. It sounds like a person talking in a studio, not like a written sentence, a translation or a pun.
- [ ] 15. The industry's own terms where they exist, and plain words for finance mechanics that aren't the point.
- [ ] 16. No formulas or shorthand: no "כן או לא:", no "אפשר להיות בוטה?", no "X או Y?" with bare nouns.
- [ ] 17. It carries its own tension: the context states the contrast or the stakes that make the question worth answering.
- [ ] 18. A confrontation uses one quote and one contradicting fact, and doesn't put words in his mouth.
- [ ] 19. No gimmicks: no stage directions, props, games, satire clips or borrowed podcast catchphrases.
- [ ] 20. It can open a clip: the likely answer starts with a number, a quote, a name or a strong feeling.

## Part 2: checks for critiquing a question

Structure

1. Count the question marks. More than one in a single bullet means a double question.
2. Look for "ו" joining two asks ("מה למדת, ואיך זה השפיע"). Split them.
3. Read the follow-ups. Each one stays on the main question's idea and doesn't open a new one.
4. Read the question right after the one before it. If the jump needs explaining, the context must explain it.
5. Check the section order follows his life, and that each question sits in the right section.
6. Check the section has two to six questions. A section with one weak question should be cut.

Context

7. Read it as a stranger. Can you say what is being asked without knowing his story?
8. Check that every name is introduced on first use with a role ("יבגני, השותף שלך").
9. Check that every company, product or term a viewer may not know is either explained in a few words or replaced.
10. Check that the context explains why the detail matters. A fact with no link ("ביום חמישי יש אצלכם ערב פיצה") fails.
11. Check that the context isn't longer than the question needs. One fact, one number, one date.
12. Check for numbers without meaning ("800 אלף ל-80 אלף") that make the viewer ask "of what?".
13. Check for stories that need an interviewer note to be understood. If the host needs a note, the viewer is lost too.

Facts

14. Find the source for every fact in the question.
15. Check the fact is about this guest and not someone with the same name.
16. Check who said each quote. If the co-founder said it, the question can't say "אמרת".
17. Check that two facts from two sources haven't been merged into one claim ("למדת מהספר בגיל 12").
18. Check every date and figure against the guest-data file.
19. Remove unsourced numbers ("עשרות חברות"). A general framing is fine.
20. Remove press attributions read aloud ("לפי ynet") when the fact is settled. Say the fact.

Assumptions

21. Underline every word that presumes something: "מתי הרגשת", "מה חיפשת", "מה עצר אותך", "מה הכי". For each, find where he said it.
22. If he never said it, turn it into an open question ("איך הרגשת").
23. Check the question doesn't presume he told someone, regrets something, or felt something.
24. Check it doesn't presume a hidden truth behind an official story unless he has said so in public.

Freshness

25. Search the transcripts of his previous interviews for the same question.
26. If he has answered it, either cut it or ask the next step: where it comes from, who saw it, what it cost.
27. Prefer what he mentioned in passing and nobody followed up on.
28. Drop questions whose famous answer everyone already knows, unless the question changes the angle.

Specificity

29. Swap in another founder's name. If the question still works, it is generic.
30. Cut advice-for-viewers questions ("מה כל צופה צריך לעשות מחר בבוקר?").
31. Cut big-topic questions any expert could answer (AI and jobs, should young people study).
32. Replace "מה היה הרגע הכי קשה" with the actual moment ("ספר לי על הבוקר שנרשמת בלשכת התעסוקה").
33. Replace "מה למדת" and "מי צדק" with a scene or a decision.

Tone and respect

34. Remove anything about his personal money: payouts, purchases, net worth, "is it true you got X".
35. Remove questions that mock him or replay satire about him.
36. Remove comparisons that put him against a named peer as a verdict.
37. Remove snark ("Armis הייתה אחת מהן?") and condescension ("איך זה שיש לך בוס").
38. Keep family questions only where he has opened the door, and phrase them his way.
39. Remove second-hand remarks that make him look bad or vain ("ראיתי אותך וויתרתי").
40. Remove questions he can't answer because of NDAs, censorship or a listed employer.
41. Remove disaster forecasts ("כמה קרובים אנחנו לבית חולים מושבת").

Language

42. Read it aloud. If nobody would say it that way in a studio, rewrite it.
43. Remove puns and clever twists ("לחדר הישיבות", "איך פיצה מאחרת הופכת לחברה").
44. Remove written register ("נולד מ", "בלי שאיש ידע") and translated English ("קח אותי לרגע").
45. Use the industry's terms (CISO, אבטחת IoT, הנפקה) and plain words for finance mechanics ("לא רצה אותם כמשקיעים").
46. Remove formulas: "כן או לא:", "אפשר להיות בוטה?", "אז מה שאתה אומר זה...".
47. Check a follow-up doesn't copy the main question's wording ("מה רצית ב-20? ומה רצית ב-30?"). Ask what went through his head at the moment instead.
48. Write either/or questions in full ("לעבוד בגוגל או לעבוד בסטארטאפ?").

Payoff

49. Check the question has tension: a contrast, a cost, a turning point.
50. Check the likely answer can stand alone as a clip.
51. Mark exactly one question in the file as the central question, and check it is the strongest one.
52. When a fact contains a surprise (a jump, a reversal, a big gap), ask about the surprise itself and not a side detail of it.
53. Use natural spoken word order and verbs. Avoid fronting the object ("את הרעיון קיבלת") and avoid repeating the same noun twice in one question.
54. Speed-round and agree/disagree items must have an answer we can't predict from his past interviews, stay away from personal money, and come from his own life.
55. After cutting questions, recount every section. A section left with one question needs refilling from the guest's own material, or merging into another section.
56. Count the statements before the question mark. More than one sentence about the guest, or any premise that starts with "אמרת ש" when the question works without it, makes the question sound like a cross-examination. Default to an open question ("איך...?", "ספר לי...") and let him tell the story; add one short, neutral premise only when the question makes no sense without it. Don't tell him his own punchline before he says it.
57. An open question still needs a concrete hook. "איזה ילד היית?" or "ספר על עצמך" is open but empty. Build the question on something true and specific about him, without reciting it as a statement.
58. Avoid yes/no follow-ups that dead-end. A follow-up should stay in the scene the main question opened and ask for the next moment or the feeling in it.
59. Check that the question says what it is really asking. If the interesting part lives only in our heads (here: the series is his own life, played by an actor), put that link into the question.
60. Ask the question the topic deserves. For each topic, ask what the guest cares about most there (for a book or series: why he made it and what he wants people to take from it) before reaching for a novelty angle.
61. Every quote needs its referent. If a quoted phrase ("הבלוף", "יש הטוענים") only makes sense with the original interview in mind, add a few words saying what it was about.
62. Watch for English calques. "איך נראה X" (what did X look like), "קח אותי ל" (take me to), "מה עובר לך" in the wrong tense: say "איך היה", "איך עבר עליך", "ספר לי על".
63. Use the spoken conditional. "אילו" plus past tense ("אילו הלכת") is literary; write "אם היית" plus a verb ("אם היית הולך").
64. Prefer the real decision to the hypothetical. "What would have happened if..." gets a guess; "how did you decide to..." gets a story he lived.
